Since there is an odd number of values, the median is the $\frac{n+1}{2}$-th term after sorting, which is the 5th term. Hence, the median is 110.

110.0

Both the manual calculation and Python give a median of 110.00 mm Hg. This makes sense as with an odd number of observations, the median is simply the middle value once the data is sorted, which is exactly what np.median() computes under the hood.

(c) Suppose we are now told that the measurement machine was not zero-ed, and that everyone's reading should be reduced by 10 mm Hg. What is the new mean and variance of the dataset? Does the mean change? Does the variance change?

Conceptually, if value is reduced by a constant $c$, which is 10 here, then for the new dataset will have the mean reduced by the same constant. Variance should remain unchanged because variance measures spread around the mean, not the mean itself. Shifting every value by the same constant shifts the mean by that constant too. So each deviation stays exactly the same after the shift, since both the data and the mean move by the same amount. No spread is added or removed, the whole dataset slides together.

In [6]:
bp_adjusted = [x - 10 for x in bp] #using a for loop to change every element

#You can use a np array instead of a list to make it simpler
bp_array = np.array(bp)
bp_adjusted = bp_array - 10

new_mean = np.mean(bp_adjusted)
new_variance = np.var(bp_adjusted, ddof=1)

print(round(new_mean, 2))
print(round(new_variance, 2))

101.67
149.75

The changes in mean and variance is in line with what we mentioned earlier.
