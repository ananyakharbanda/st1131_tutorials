Question 1¶

An exam consists of 50 multiple choice questions. Based on how much you studied, for any given question you think you have a probability of $p = 0.7$ of getting the correct answer. Consider the sampling distribution of the sample proportion $\hat{p}$ of the 50 questions on which you get the correct answer.

(a) Find the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of this proportion.

Each question is either correct with probability $p=0.7$ or wrong. Sample size is $n=50$.

The sampling distribution of the sample proportion, $\hat{p}$ has mean $0.7$ and standard deviation of:

$$ \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{(0.3)(0.7)}{50}} \approx 0.0648 $$

(b) What do you expect for the shape of the sampling distribution? Why?

Since sample size $n=50$ and $np(1-p) = 50(0.3)(0.7) = 10.5$ is large enough, by CLT, the distribution of $\hat{p}$ can be approximated by a normal distribution.

(c) If true $p = 0.7$, then would it be surprising if you got the correct answer on at most 60% of the questions?

If true $p=0.7$ then

$$ \hat{p} \sim N\left(0.7, \frac{(0.3)(0.7)}{50}\right) $$

The probability of getting at most 60% correct answer is:

$$ P(\hat{p} \le 0.6) = P\left(Z \le \frac{0.6 - 0.7}{0.0648}\right) = P(Z \le -1.543) = 0.0614 $$

Since this probability is about 0.06, getting at most 60% correct would be somewhat unusual but not very surprising.

Question 2¶

A supplier of electronic chips for tablets claims that only 4% of his chips are defective. A manufacturer tests 500 randomly selected chips from a large shipment from the supplier for potential defects.

(a) Find the mean and standard deviation for the distribution of the sample proportion of defective chips in the sample of 500.

Each chip is either defective with probability $0.04$ or not defective. Sample size is $n=500$.

The proportion of defective chips in a sample is $\hat{p}$ which has distribution with mean $0.04$ and standard deviation of:

$$ \sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}} = \sqrt{\frac{(0.04)(0.96)}{500}} \approx 0.00876 $$

(b) Is it reasonable to assume a normal shape for the sampling distribution? Explain.

It is reasonable to assume a normal shape for the sampling distribution of $\hat{p}$, since the sample size $n=500$ and $np(1-p) = 500(0.04)(0.96) = 19.2$ is large enough.

(c) The manufacturer will return the entire shipment if he finds at least 5% of the 500 sampled chips to be defective.

Find the theoretical probability that the shipment will be returned, and use scipy.stats.norm to compute this probability in Python.

If true $p=0.04$, then

$$ \hat{p} \sim N\left(0.04, \frac{(0.04)(0.96)}{500}\right) $$

The shipment will be returned if from the sample we obtain a $\hat{p}$ that is 5% or larger. Hence, the probability that the shipment is returned is:

$$ P(\hat{p} \ge 0.05) = P\left(Z \ge \frac{0.05 - 0.04}{\sqrt{\frac{(0.04)(0.96)}{500}}}\right) = P(Z \ge 1.141) = 0.1269 $$

Question 3¶

According to a recent Current Population Report, the population distribution of the number of years of education for self-employed individuals in the US has a mean of 13.6 and a standard deviation of 3.

(a) Identify the random variable $X$ whose population distribution is described here.

The random variable $X$ is the number of years of education for self-employed US individuals.

(b) Find the mean and standard deviation of the sampling distribution of $\bar{X}$ for a random sample of size $n = 100$. Interpret the results.

The mean is $13.6$ and the standard deviation is:

$$ \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{100}} = 0.3 $$

The mean of the sampling distribution is $13.6$, the same as the mean of the population.

The standard deviation of the sampling distribution is smaller than the standard deviation of the population. It reflects the variability of the sample mean from samples of size $100$ taken from this population.

(c) Repeat part (b) for $n = 400$. Describe the effect of increasing $n$.

The mean of the sampling distribution is still $13.6$ and the standard deviation is:

$$ \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{400}} = 0.15 $$

As $n$ increases, the mean of the sampling distribution stays the same, but the standard deviation gets smaller.
